# Gear that fits every body (Look v2)

Gear is defined once and built on the wearer's own body, so a Knollfolk's breastplate fits a Knollfolk and the same
breastplate on a 2.1 m Larchfolk fits the Larchfolk (as gear does in FFXI or Warcraft). Code:
`client/src/player/bodySurface.ts` (the body's surfaces), `gearFit.ts` (the fitter), `gearDefs.ts` (every look as
data), `gearModels.ts` (look ids, plan, cache, hooks), tests `gearFit.test.ts` and `gearModels.test.ts`, dev page
`client/dev/gearfit.html` (`gearFitDev.ts`).

## How a piece fits

- **One source of truth.** `bodySurface.ts` holds the body's rows (torso superellipse sections with bust, pectorals,
  belly and seat; arms; legs with the boot-top cut; neck) as functions of `bodyFrame(look)`. `body2.ts` draws the body
  from them, and the fitter offsets gear from exactly the same surface, so a piece neither clips nor floats.
- **Landmarks.** Heights are named stations (`ST`: ankle, calf, knee, thigh, crotch, hip, waist, ribs, chest,
  shoulder, shoulder top, neck, chin; `AST` on the arm: cap, shoulder, bicep, elbow, forearm, wrist, hand). A piece
  says "from 0.3 below the hip to the neck", never metres, so it lands in the same place on every body.
- **Primitives** (`Fit`): `shell` (a surface over a region at an offset along the body's normal, with lips turning back
  to the body at open edges; its rows include the body's own rows where the body bulges past the room the offset
  leaves, and its angles are the body's ring angles, so no chord cuts inside), `band`, `skirt` (hangs round the legs:
  its radius by direction is the lower body's support plus clearance), `cape` (down the back, clear of the back, seat
  and legs; hangs from under pauldrons; a Brindlekin's tail leaves by a slit), `panel` (a tabard: bridges a bust),
  `pauldron` (a dome sized to enclose the shoulder, with lames), `hands`/`feet` (the body's own hand and foot grown),
  `headShell` (over the skull from the crown to an edge by azimuth: face opening, cheek guards, nape; on the hair and
  beard where it sits on them; holes for Larchfolk and Brindlekin ears), `headRing`, `brim` (parted by standing ears),
  `frame`/`headFrame`/`paulFrame` (ornaments anchored to a place, scaled with the body), `strap`.
- **What a piece covers** is left out of the body (`BodyCover`: torso rows, arm/leg/neck heights, hands, feet; hair
  `full`/`cap`/`tuck`/`none` and round ears under hoods and helms): no skin is drawn under armour, and the triangles
  saved pay for the gear.
- **Hit radius.** Every gear vertex's |x| is held softly inside `ADVENTURER_RADIUS` (`softClampX`).
- **Weapons and off-hands** ride their joints, scaled with the wearer about the grip (`weaponScale`: height^0.9 ×
  hand^0.15), so a Knollfolk's greatsword is in proportion and both grips stay on it.

## Slots, seams and layering

Each slot draws only its region: head; chest (torso, upper arms and shoulders as the piece defines: sleeves none / cap /
short / elbow / long / bell); hands (gloves or gauntlets to the forearm); legs (seat and legs, hips to shins); feet
(boots, their shafts up the shin); back (cape, cloak, pack, wings). Pieces are fitted in the order
**legs → feet → chest → hands → back → head**, and each shell is lifted over what is already worn there (`LAYER_GAP`):

- the chest's hem goes over the trousers' waistband (the belt line is the chest piece's);
- boots go over leg armour and trousers on the shin;
- gloves go over a fitted sleeve; under a loose (bell) sleeve the cuff stays inside it;
- a cape goes over everything on the back and hangs below pauldrons; a hood or helm goes over collars.

Bare slots show the body's undergarment (a linen shirt to the hips, braies, low shoes): never a hole. An empty back
under any armour shows the job's own cape fitted over the gear; a hidden back shows none.

## Looks as data

A piece is one row of `DEFS` in `gearDefs.ts`:

```ts
[id, slot, class, tier, template, params, modules, palette, glow?]
```

- **template**: a base silhouette per slot — chest `garment` (tunic, jerkin, robe, coat, gown) and `cuirass`; legs
  `trousers` (with mail, cuisses, greaves, tassets, knee pads or cops); hands `gloves` (wraps, gloves, gauntlets);
  feet `boots` (shoes, slippers, boots, sabatons); head `hood`, `cap` (caps, wraps, hats with brims), `helm`, `band`
  (circlets, goggles), `halo`; back `cape`, `pack`, `wings`.
- **params**: the template's knobs (`hem`, `skirt`, `open`, `sleeve`, `bell`, `neck`, `pattern`, `paul`, `plate`, …;
  see each template's comment).
- **modules**: trims and ornaments in order (`belt`, `sash`, `tabard`, `strap`, `leafHem`, `embers`, `lens`, `thorns`,
  `fringe`, `buckles`, `bells`, `tails`, `capelet`, `rivets`, `cogs`, `fins`, `roots`, `crest`, `stars`, `buttons`,
  `rays`, `ruff`, `apron`, `lamp`, `kerchief`, `patches`, `helmTop`, `goggles`, `spikes`, `cuffBand`, `handPlates`,
  `laces`).
- **palette**: own colours `[primary, secondary, trim]` — the three dye channels; dye 0 = these.

`gearFit.test.ts` checks every look id in shared `ITEMS` and `COSTUME_PIECES` has a row of its slot with a distinct
signature and distinct geometry, so a new look id without a row (or a copy of another) fails.

### Worked example 1: a new cloth chest piece (look id 61, "Fernweave Robe", T1)

```ts
[61, 'chest', 'cloth', 1, 'garment',
  { hem: 5.4, skirt: 2.4, sleeve: 'bell', bell: 0.03, neck: 'wrap', off: 0.009, pattern: 'panel' },
  [['leafHem', { u: 2.5, n: 14 }], ['sash', { col: 'c' }], ['tabard', { w: 0.75, to: 2.8 }]],
  [shade(P.meadow, 0.7), mix(P.meadow, P.cloud, 0.4), P.gold]],
```

A calf-length robe (`skirt: 2.4` = between calf and knee) with bell sleeves, a wrap neckline, side panels in the
secondary colour, a leaf hem in the trim, a sash and the job's stole. It fits every body with no other code: run
`npx vitest run src/player/gearFit.test.ts` and look at `dev/gearfit.html?view=slot&slot=chest`.

### Worked example 2: a new heavy helm (look id 62, "Gatewarden Helm", T2)

```ts
[62, 'head', 'heavy', 2, 'helm',
  { front: 1.1, side: 2.2, back: 2.4, off: 0.018, crown: 0.02, ribs: true, nasal: true },
  [['helmTop', { kind: 'plume', col: 'b' }]],
  [M.steel, P.roof, M.brass]],
```

An open-faced helm whose cheek guards come down to the jaw (`side: 2.2` radians from the crown), ribbed, with a nasal
and a red plume. As a `helm` it hides all hair and covers round ears; Larchfolk and Brindlekin ears come through holes;
its cheek guards stand off a beard. A new *kind* of piece is a new template or module function (a few dozen lines on the
`Fit` primitives); a new look of an existing kind is one row.

## Performance

- One skinned mesh per adventurer (plus the existing emissive mesh only for lit T3 pieces and named weapons; nothing
  below T3 glows).
- Fitted pieces are cached per (body shape × look × detail × the pieces under it × dyes), LRU-bounded
  (`GEAR_CACHE_SIZE` 384); the whole adventurer stays cached by look and gear code (`BODY_CACHE_SIZE`); rebuilds are
  queued (`REBUILDS_PER_FRAME`).
- Triangles (16 random looks × the jobs, full T3 vs no gear, `gearModels.test.ts`): low 1.78k → 2.19k (×1.23 mean,
  ×1.41 worst), med 2.82k → 3.42k (×1.21, ×1.32), high 6.17k → 6.26k (×1.01, ×1.16); far T3 mean 1.58k, worst 1.72k.
  Detail rules: the smallest ornaments (rivets, studs, bells, stitches, buttons) only at high; the far build keeps only
  silhouette pieces (no lips, straps, over-skirts).
