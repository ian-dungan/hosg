# Look v2: a detailed, human character builder (shared rules + server)

2026-11-16. The owner asked for "a much more detailed character builder; the player should be able to build
characters that look like anyone; male and female should be choices". The game keeps its low-poly style but
moves to natural human proportions with shaped faces ("stylised, more human"). This spec is the data: what a
look can express, how it is validated, encoded, upgraded from v1 and served. The client model and creator are
rebuilt separately on top of it.

Code: `shared/src/look.ts` (v2), `shared/src/lookV1.ts` (v1, kept for stored looks and old codes),
`shared/src/lookCommon.ts` (peoples, palettes helpers, base64url), `shared/src/gearLook.ts` (the gear look,
unchanged, moved out of look.ts). Tests: `look.test.ts`, `lookV1.test.ts`, `gearLook.test.ts`.

## Rules

1. **Body**: `'male' | 'female'`, a base body shape. Every slider and choice is open to both (facial hair,
   make-up, every hair style). Looks only: no stat, hitbox, speed or reach difference. `look.test.ts` checks
   that nothing in `combat/`, `movement.ts`, `body.ts`, `kit.ts`, `progression.ts`, `talents.ts` or
   `items.ts` imports a look module or names `Look`.
2. **Peoples** keep their v1 data (`PEOPLES`: heights, spans, ear counts, fur, suggested colour ranges) and
   features on top of the body: ear style (`ears.style`, up to `earsMax`), fur pattern and colour, tail and
   muzzle (furred peoples only).
3. **No free text** anywhere in a look: every field is an integer, a colour, or one of the fixed ids.
4. **Colours** are 0xRRGGBB with a lightness clamp: luma (0.299 R + 0.587 G + 0.114 B) within
   [`LUMA_MIN` = 16, `LUMA_MAX` = 250]. `parseLook` refuses a colour outside it; `clampLookColour` moves one
   inside (all three channels by the same step, capped at 0 and 255; unchanged if already inside;
   idempotent). The creator should offer the clamped colour. Every palette entry is inside the clamp. The
   client may clamp further for rendering (its `renderColour`), never less.
5. **Strict parsing**: `parseLook` gives a fresh copy or null; exact keys at every level, integers in range,
   known ids, the ears within the people's, fur exactly for a furred people, no extra keys. It never throws
   (getters and proxies that throw read as null). `readLook` reads v2, or a valid v1 look upgraded.

## The code

Every field is one digit of a mixed-radix number, most significant first: the version (radix 16, = 2), the
people (4), the body (2), then each group's fields in `Look` order (an integer's radix is its range, a colour's
2^24, an optional colour's 2^24 + 1 with 0 = null; fur digits 0 for peoples without fur). The number is
written as 54 big-endian bytes and then base64url without padding: 72 characters. Decoding is strict: the code
must be exactly what `encodeLook` writes for a valid look. Versions are told apart by length: 38 characters is
v1 (decoded with `decodeLookV1` and upgraded), 72 is v2, anything else is refused. The code is sent once per
adventurer per client (the `looks` message) and on a change.

## v1 → v2

`upgradeLookV1` keeps the people, every body slider (v1 `build` becomes `weight`; chest, waist, arms, muscle
and neck 10), the ears, the hair style and facial hair (the v1 numbers are the first v2 entries), the eyes and
brows (likewise), stance, walk and dyes, and every colour (lightness-clamped; the clamp moves only near-black
and near-white colours). The v1 face shape, nose and mouth become slider presets (`FACE_SHAPE_PRESETS`,
`NOSE_PRESETS`, `MOUTH_PRESETS`); for the Brindlekin the v1 nose is also the muzzle. The v1 mark becomes
freckles (2), a left or right cheek scar, the upper-lip mole, or face paint (cheek bands, dots, brow paint) in
the mark's colour. New choices sit at their middle or none; age 0.

**Body**: `'female'` only when the v1 look has no facial hair and hips at least 4 steps wider than shoulders
(about 15% of random and of default v1 looks), else `'male'`. The v1 model had no body type, so this is a guess; it only
matters until the adventurer's free visit to the new creator.

**Default looks** (an adventurer who never used the creator): `defaultLook(seed)` is the Plan 5 default
upgraded, so nobody's adventurer changes unasked. `randomLook` (bots, "Surprise me") samples around the presets.

`lookV1View(look)` maps any v2 look back to a valid v1 look (nearest presets, v1 fallbacks): the pre-v2 client
draws from it until it is rebuilt, and `lookV1View(upgradeLookV1(l))` is `l` with colours clamped.

`mergeLookV1(current, v1Look)`: the pre-v2 creator sends v1 looks; the server applies only what changed (each
v1 field to the v2 fields it maps to) onto the current look, keeping the body and every v2-only detail. A new
people takes the whole upgrade, keeping the body. So a v1 Barber edit costs what it did (40, or 200 for a new
people) and never flips the body.

## Storage and the free visit

No destructive migration and no new migration. The `look` jsonb keeps whatever it holds; reads upgrade a v1
look on the fly (`readLook`); every save writes v2 (`writeInventory` and `useFreeLook` take only v2). Every
adventurer whose stored look is v1 gets one free visit to the new creator: the stores read the free flag as
`look_free OR look->>'v' = '1'` (`lookFreeOf`, `storedLookIsV1`), and `useFreeLook` updates under the same
condition. Saving a v2 look (the free visit, or the Barber) clears it for good, so the rule is idempotent.
Adventurers with no stored look already have their free visit.

## Server

The `look` message and `POST /api/look` take a v2 look, or a v1 look merged as above. Rate limits unchanged.
The largest v2 look is about 1.2 kB of JSON, under the route's 2 kB body limit.
