# The wardrobe: an appearance collection, per-piece looks, three dye channels and saved outfits

2026-11-16. The owner: "Characters and their gear and costumes they win will be very important, so let's have a
high level of control over appearance", and "Each piece should be interchangeable. Not just a generic look."
This is the data and the server rules; the client draws it. Appearance only: stats always come from the item
really worn (`kitFor` reads none of this; `wardrobe.test.ts` checks it over random operation sequences).

Code: `shared/src/wardrobe.ts` (look ids, the collection, per-slot appearance), `shared/src/costumes.ts`
(costume pieces), `shared/src/gearLook.ts` (the v2 gear code), `shared/src/inventory.ts` (the operations).

## Rules

1. **Look ids.** Every item keeps its own model (0..199, one per item: no two items share a look) and every
   costume piece has its own (200..255). `LOOKS` lists them all with slot, tier, armour type, set or costume.
   Each slot has as many distinct looks as it has items and costume pieces (a table test).
2. **The collection.** One bit per look id (32 bytes, base64url in the inventory's `looks`). A look is unlocked
   for good when the adventurer holds the item (bag, worn, Vault: bought, looted, a quest reward, Seals, a GM
   grant; even a drop sold at once because the bag was full) or owns the costume piece. Nothing ever clears a
   bit: selling or replacing an item keeps its look. It is per adventurer, for every job. Stored inside the
   inventory jsonb, so it is written in the same compare-and-set as the change that unlocks it. Back-fill: on
   every read the bits for everything held are added (the first read of a pre-wardrobe inventory unlocks its bag,
   gear, Vault and costumes, and is written back once); doing it again changes nothing.
3. **Per-slot looks** (`look` op): each of the eight slots may show any unlocked look that fits it: an armour
   slot any armour look of that slot, of any armour type, or a costume piece of that slot; the weapon slot a
   weapon look of the job's weapon type; the off-hand an off-hand look of its kind. Freely mixed, piece by piece:
   head from one set, chest from another, gloves from a costume, boots from a third. `null` = the worn piece's
   own. A slot with nothing really worn (a two-hander's off-hand) shows nothing. Per job.
4. **Hiding** (`hide` op): head, back (cape) and off-hand (sheathed) only; there is no shoulder slot.
5. **Dyes**: each slot has three channels (primary, secondary, trim) from the 32 dyes (0 = the piece's own
   colours). A slot without its own dyes shows the piece's: an item's two dyes (the `dye` op, as before) or a
   costume's (`dyeCostume`, as before), trim 0. **Economy kept as found**: dyes are not owned items; each channel
   change costs `DYE_PRICE` = 5 gold at the Tailor, the same dye again is free (`dyeSlot` op).
6. **Costume pieces.** A body costume is four pieces (chest, legs, hands, feet: the chest keeps the costume's
   model), a head or back costume one. Owning a costume owns all its pieces (existing owners are converted on
   read: idempotent). Pieces are bought one at a time or the whole costume at once (the pieces not yet owned, at
   their prices): `buyCostume` takes a costume id or a piece id. Acquisition is unchanged otherwise: gold at the
   Tailor or a GM/event grant; no boss-drop costumes. `wardrobe` (whole costumes owned) is kept for older readers.
7. **Silhouette cue**: a head look may hide the job's silhouette cue (heavy helms do, `LOOKS[id].hidesCue`); the
   gear code carries `cueHidden`. Data only.
8. **Where**: `look` and `hide` anywhere out of combat (alive, not busy), free; refused inside a sealed boss arena
   like gear changes (ruling P5-h). `dyeSlot` at the Tailor. Never trusted from the client: ownership of the look
   (refusal `locked`), slot fit (`fit`), ranges (`unknown`).
9. **The pre-wardrobe outfit** (a job's `outfit`: body/head/back costume, hideHead) is read as its pieces until the
   job's appearance is first changed; every appearance change also writes the closest old outfit, so an older
   client still shows whole costumes. The old `wear` and `hideHead` ops still work (on the appearance).

## Saved outfits

Up to `MAX_OUTFITS` = 6 per job. Each is a label from `OUTFIT_LABELS` (Everyday, Adventuring, Town, Festival,
Travel, Formal, Hunting, Night, Rain, Stealth, Ceremony, Work; an index, never free text) and the per-slot looks,
dyes and hides. `saveOutfit {index, label}` stores the job's current appearance (free); `wearOutfit {index}` puts
it on (free, anywhere out of combat; refused in combat, knocked out, busy and inside a sealed boss arena, like gear
changes; a look not allowed shows the piece's own, never trusted); `deleteOutfit {index}`. Dyes in an outfit were
paid for when they were set, so wearing an outfit costs nothing. Stored in the inventory (`outfits`), cleaned on
read (a bad label or entry is empty, a locked or ill-fitting look is dropped).

## Operations and the view

| op | fields | where | cost | refusals beyond dead/combat/busy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `look` | `slot` (8 slots), `look`: look id 0..255 or null | anywhere; not in a sealed arena | free | `unknown` (not a look), `locked`, `fit` |
| `hide` | `slot`: head, back, offhand; `hide` | anywhere; not in a sealed arena | free | |
| `dyeSlot` | `slot`, `ch` 0..2, `dye` 0..31 | Tailor | 5 gold a change, same dye free | `where`, `gold` |
| `buyCostume` | `costume`: a costume id or a piece id | Tailor (op id needed) | the pieces not yet owned | `unknown` (event only), `owned`, `gold` |
| `saveOutfit` | `index` 0..5, `label` 0..11 | anywhere | free | |
| `wearOutfit` | `index` 0..5 | anywhere; not in a sealed arena | free | `unknown` (empty) |
| `deleteOutfit` | `index` 0..5 | anywhere | free | |

`InventoryView` (the owner's `inventory` message only) gains `looks` (unlocked look ids), `appearance` (the current
job's eight slots, each `{ look, dyes, hide }`) and `outfits` (six places, null = empty).

## The gear code v2

A byte of which slots show, a flag byte (bit 7 set = v2; bits 0–2 hidden head, back, off-hand; bit 3 cue hidden),
then per shown slot in slot order the look id (8 bits) and three dyes (5 bits each), bit-packed, base64url without
padding: **at most 25 bytes, 34 characters** (v1: at most 20 bytes, 27 characters). Sent in the `looks` message as
before (once per adventurer per client, and on a change). v1 codes still decode (`decodeGearLookV2` upgrades them:
dyes [a, b, 0], hideHead → hidden head); `decodeGearLook` reads a v2 code as its v1 view for the pre-wardrobe
client (two dyes; costume pieces in legs, hands and feet not shown; a body costume's top covers legs, hands and
feet as before).
